# Article and Companion Review

## Source

Miner, M. J. (2025). "When the Output Looks Like Learning." *i.e.: inquiry in education*, 18(1), Article 4. https://digitalcommons.nl.edu/ie/vol18/iss1/4/

## Core Argument

The article argues that generative AI can produce artifacts that look like learning without requiring the learner to perform the underlying cognitive work. The problem is not simply that a student may submit AI-generated text. The deeper issue is that schools often infer learning from polished symbolic products, while generative AI can now produce those products on demand.

The companion site makes that argument legible by placing the reader inside a familiar assessment scene: a student essay appears proficient, but the apparent performance may not reflect comprehension, struggle, revision, or conceptual growth.

## Strengths

- The classroom scenario makes an abstract AI-governance concern concrete for teachers and school leaders.
- The media-ecology frame gives the argument more depth than a basic cheating-or-detection discussion.
- The distinction between performance and learning is especially useful for assessment design.
- The friction language is practical: it helps separate productive learning struggle from unnecessary barriers.
- The companion format supports readers who may need a visual, staged entry point before engaging the full article.

## Repository Recommendations

- Keep the companion site static and non-collecting.
- Link clearly to the official article page rather than duplicating the article text.
- Use GitHub Pages from `main` at the repository root.
- Preserve the article citation in the README so the companion remains academically traceable.
- Avoid adding analytics or embedded third-party widgets unless there is a clear reason and a privacy review.

## Pre-Publication Checklist

- Confirm the Digital Commons article URL resolves publicly.
- Preview `index.html` in a browser.
- Check desktop and mobile layout.
- Verify keyboard navigation for major interactive elements.
- Confirm that all assets load from the local `assets/` folder.
- After push, enable GitHub Pages and test the live URL directly.
